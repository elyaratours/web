The Cathedral and Royal Chapel tell the story of Granada's new political and religious project after the Christian conquest. This route focuses on their spaces, symbols and protagonists to read the city through power, art and dynastic memory.

We talk about the Catholic Monarchs, the transformation of the city center, Renaissance architecture and the role of these monuments as the stage for a Granada that wanted to present itself differently to the world. The visit helps explain why the historic center cannot be separated from 1492, the new image of the monarchy and the overlapping memories that still appear in the streets.

The Royal Chapel opens questions about burial, legitimacy, artistic collections and political propaganda. The Cathedral offers another reading: the ambition to build a monumental Christian city over an inherited urban fabric, with spaces that are not only beautiful but also messages of authority.

This route is especially useful if you have already visited, or plan to visit, the Alhambra and want to complete Granada's story from another angle. It also suits travelers interested in art, urban history, symbols of power and private visits at a calmer rhythm.
